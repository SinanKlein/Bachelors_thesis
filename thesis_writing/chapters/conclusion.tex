After these experiments, we can conclude that no shared latent space unifying $y$ and $w_{\text{class}}$ in
terms of prediction has emerged. The joint model matches the single-task network on $y$, and its small gain on
$w_{\text{class}}$ survives permuting the $y$ labels (Table~\ref{tab:shuffle-y}), so what it learns is a
latent space for $y$ with a second head attached rather than a representation serving both.

Beyond the two targets, the pattern suggests something about the approach. The pipeline uses no functional
annotation: the protein clusters are formed by sequence similarity without functional labelling, the features
are chosen by variance, and the models are generic. The single place where explicit biological knowledge
enters is the $Y$ label, which records a mechanism rather than an association, and that is the single place
the pipeline performs well. $W$ has no comparable biological prior. It is the output of an estimator applied
to abundances \citep{friedman2008,liu2010}, and nothing in the features or the objective constrains it
towards infection rather than towards any other reason two organisms might covary.
