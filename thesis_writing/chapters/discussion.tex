\subsection{Interpretation}
\label{disc:interpretation}
The weak signal on the $W$ task barely responds to any of the remedies tried: added capacity helps
inconsistently (Section~\ref{res:main-wclass}), no arm improves $w_{\text{class}}$ in any cohort
(Section~\ref{res:ablation}), no interior cut of $W$ exceeds 0.62 AUC (Figure~\ref{fig:wthreshold}), and
family-level aggregation does not help (Section~\ref{res:taxres}). This could be because of a structural
mismatch. $Y_{ij}$ records a physical event between two genomes and is for that reason a property of the pair
alone, so the biology that defines it also makes it representable from $x_i$ and $x_j$. $W_{ij}$ has no such
definition: it is an entry of a regularised precision matrix and depends on every other organism in the
community, so no function of $x_i$ and $x_j$ represents it exactly. This is a conjecture rather than a tested
claim, and the consistent above-chance signal makes it a bound rather than a barrier. A second alternative is
that $W$ does not measure interaction at all, supported by the targets overlapping little more than independence
predicts (Section~\ref{data}) and by abundance correlations recovering known infection networks at close to
chance level \citep{coenen2018}, a limitation reported for microbial association networks more generally
\citep{pinto2022}.

\subsection{Limitations and future work}
\label{disc:limitations}
Four limitations bound these results. The folds are drawn over pairs rather than organisms
(Section~\ref{sec:eval}), so every genus and vOTU appears in training and only the combination is new at test
time; what is measured is the prediction of unobserved pairings among known organisms, not host prediction for
an unseen phage. CRISPR linkage is a lower bound on interaction, since a missing spacer does not establish
that no infection occurred \citep{zhang2021spacepharer}, so the negative class is unreliable. Predictions are
made at genus and vOTU level, while host range is frequently strain-specific. And every model uses one fixed
hyperparameter setting (Section~\ref{setup}), chosen to keep the arms comparable rather than to maximise
performance.

Two directions follow from the representation. Existing approaches build their features from curated resources
\citep{edwards2016,coutinho2021,roux2023,nie2024}, whereas the one used here is annotation-free. If a
biological prior is what separates the two targets, then for $W$ the place to supply it is the features, since
the target itself cannot be redefined: selecting protein clusters by biological role, such as receptor
binding, adhesion or defence, would aim the model at the component of $W$ that a genuine infection would
produce rather than at whatever varies most. A protein language model such as ESM-2 \citep{lin2023} would
supply the same prior in transferred form, and its embeddings are shared across cohorts, which would in
addition remove the vocabulary barrier that currently prevents training in one cohort and evaluating in
another (Section~\ref{data}). How far either can go on $W$ is bounded by the mismatch of
Section~\ref{disc:interpretation}, since the part of $W_{ij}$ that depends on the rest of the community
remains beyond the reach of any pairwise representation.
