
\subsection{Cohorts}
The data come from three published studies of the human gut microbiome, covering colorectal cancer (CRC)
\citep{wirbel2019}, inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) \citep{lloydprice2019} and graft-versus-host disease
(GvHD) \citep{peled2020}. The clinical endpoints of the studies do not play a role in this thesis. The contributions
of the studies are a set of bacterial genera, a set of viral operational taxonomic units (vOTUs), and the
abundance and protein-cluster profiles of both, so that a cohort enters the analysis as the grid of candidate
phage--bacteria pairs summarised in Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Cohorts and units of analysis}
  \label{tab:cohorts}
  \begin{tabular}{lrrr}
    \hline
                       &    CRC &    IBD &   GvHD \\
    \hline
    Samples            &     84 &    148 &     90 \\
    Bacterial genera   &     75 &     94 &     95 \\
    vOTUs              &    186 &    293 &    379 \\
    Candidate pairs    & 13,950 & 27,542 & 36,005 \\
    \hline
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

We applied the same analysis to three different cohorts rather than one, because a result obtained in a single cohort
cannot be separated from the properties of that cohort. Repeating the same analysis in three datasets collected by
different groups and in different populations turns a finding that holds in all three into a statement about the
signal rather than about one dataset.

The cohorts do not share the same feature vocabulary. The protein clusters are derived within each cohort
separately, so a model fitted in one cohort reads columns that do not exist in another and cannot be transferred
to it. Every model in this thesis is therefore trained and evaluated inside a single
cohort, and the comparison between cohorts is a comparison of findings and not of transferred predictions.

\subsection{Protein-cluster representation}
\label{data:procs}
Both bacteria and viruses are described in the same way without any annotation database. The proteins predicted from
all genomes of a cohort are clustered \emph{de novo} with MMseqs2 \citep{steinegger2017}, and a genus or vOTU
is then a profile over those clusters. Nothing about the function of a cluster enters the representation, and
because clustering is repeated per cohort, the three cohorts share no feature vocabulary.

Bacteria and virus protein features exhibit different sizes. For example, in the CRC cohort, the bacterial genomes 
yield 70{,}625 clusters, a median of 3{,}765 per genus, whereas the viral genomes yield 2{,}756 clusters,
a median of 30 per vOTU.
Selection therefore keeps every viral cluster and cuts the bacterial matrix to the same width by variance, discarding 
90 to 96 per cent of it (Table~\ref{tab:proc-counts}, Figures~\ref{fig:app-featcounts} and~\ref{fig:app-featactivity}).
We score variance on raw counts so that the selection is independent of the transform applied afterwards; which transform, and whether the halves are concatenated or summed, is taken up in Section~\ref{sec:features}.

\subsection{Constructing the bipartite grid}
The rows of the grid are bacterial genera and the columns are viral operational taxonomic units, indexed by
$i$ and $j$ respectively. Because a genus or a vOTU without features cannot be passed to the model, we only take
genera and vOTUs that appear in the CRISPR table and also carry a protein-cluster profile \citep{steinegger2017}.
The grid is then the full Cartesian product of the two surviving
sets, so that every genus is paired with every vOTU and the unit of analysis is the pair rather than the
organism. This is what produces the pair counts in Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}. In IBD we dropped one
genus since it had no protein-cluster profile, whereas all genera were kept in the other two cohorts.

We place the CRISPR target on the grid directly as it is indexed by the same genera and vOTUs. It is used in
binary form, so that $Y_{ij} = 1$ if at least one spacer of genus $i$ matches vOTU $j$
\citep{zhang2021spacepharer} and $Y_{ij} = 0$ otherwise; the number of matching spacers is discarded.

The glasso matrix is estimated separately from the abundance data \citep{friedman2008,liu2010}, from $B = 50$
subsamples at an instability threshold of $\beta = 0.2$; a sparser network at $\beta = 0.1$ was also available and is
not used here. It is estimated on its own set of entities, with its own naming convention, meaning it does not
necessarily align to the grid in its raw form. We normalise the names on both sides before any matching, by
lowercasing, removing every character that is not a letter or a digit, and then reindex $W$ onto the grid. We then
fill the cells that have no genus or vOTU counterparts in the glasso matrix with zero, and record a second matrix of
the same shape marking which cells were matched. That matrix is the observation mask.

The glasso matrix is continuous. $W_{ij} \in [0,1]$ is the fraction of subsamples in which the edge between
genus $i$ and vOTU $j$ was selected, so it is a selection frequency rather than a binary statement about the
pair. During the pipeline, we use it in two forms. In the regression task we keep the value itself, and in the
classification task we threshold it at zero, so that the label is $1$ if $W_{ij} > 0$ and $0$ otherwise. Together
with the CRISPR target this gives the three prediction targets used throughout this thesis, referred to
as $y$, $w_{\text{reg}}$ and $w_{\text{class}}$.

\subsection{The observation mask}
The glasso matrix does not cover the whole grid. It is estimated on the entities that survive the prevalence
filter of the abundance data, which means it is not the same set as the entities that carry both a CRISPR entry and a
protein-cluster profile. Thus, only some of the genera and only some of the vOTUs of the grid have a counterpart in
$W$. We record this in a binary matrix $M$ of the same shape as the grid, with
$M_{ij} = 1$ if genus $i$ and vOTU $j$ were both matched and $M_{ij} = 0$ if not. A cell is matched exactly when its
row and its column are both matched, so $M$ is the outer product of the two indicator vectors and the observed
cells form a block rather than a scattered pattern. In CRC, 62 genera
and 93 vOTUs are matched, which gives $62 \times 93 = 5{,}766$ observed pairs, or 41\% of the grid.
The corresponding figures are $75 \times 70 = 5{,}250$ pairs (19\%) in IBD and $56 \times 344 = 19{,}264$
pairs (54\%) in GvHD.

Even though the unmatched cells are stored as zero on the grid, that zero is not a statement about the pair. Treating
it as an observed absence would teach the model that unmatched $W$ edges mean absence. In reality the cell is
unobserved, so it is not known from the data if the interaction was absent or present. We therefore exclude those
cells entirely from the pipeline.

Because $Y$ is defined on the whole grid, the intersection between the $Y$-observed and the $W$-observed set is the
$W$-observed set itself, and this single mask places all three tasks on the same pairs. The whole pipeline is applied
to an identical set of pairs, meaning a difference between the $Y$ task models, $W$ task models and joint models is a
difference between the targets and not between the data they were shown.

The cost is that $Y$ is no longer evaluated on the full grid. In CRC, 5,766 of the 13,950 pairs remain and the
CRISPR prevalence rises slightly from 3.1\% to 3.4\%, so the $Y$ results describe the masked subset and not
the complete genus $\times$ vOTU grid.

\subsection{Descriptive statistics} Unless stated otherwise, the numbers in this subsection refer to the masked set,
since that is the set every model is trained and evaluated on. Table~\ref{tab:descriptives} collects them. The two
tasks differ greatly in terms of their positive classes. CRISPR linkage marks between 2\% and 4\% of the observed
pairs, while a glasso edge is present in roughly two out of five, which means a model faces a strong imbalance on $Y$
and a nearly balanced one on $W$. The class balance on the $W$ task is a consequence of the thresholding rather than a
property of the network. $W$ is a selection frequency, so $W > 0$ admits every pair that was selected in at least one
of the fifty subsamples, whereas the glasso network at the selected penalty has a bacteria--virus density of only 2\%
to 4\% across the three cohorts.

    \hline
    Observed pairs            &  5,766 &  5,250 & 19,264 \\
    CRISPR positive           & 198 (3.4\%) & 182 (3.5\%) & 478 (2.5\%) \\
    Glasso edge ($W > 0$)     & 2,473 (42.9\%) & 2,019 (38.5\%) & 9,451 (49.1\%) \\
    Mean $W$                  &  0.083 &  0.097 &  0.101 \\

The two targets also overlap far less than their prevalences alone would suggest. If CRISPR linkage and a
glasso edge were independent of each other, the expected number of pairs carrying both would be
%
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:expected-overlap}
  \mathbb{E}[\,\text{both}\,] = \frac{n_Y \, n_W}{n_{\text{obs}}} ,
\end{equation}
%
with $n_Y$ and $n_W$ the number of positives of each target and $n_{\text{obs}}$ the number of observed pairs.
In CRC this gives $198 \times 2{,}473 / 5{,}766 = 85$, against the 84 pairs that actually carry both. The
corresponding figures are 92 observed against 70 expected in IBD and 265 against 235 in GvHD. The two signals are
therefore close to independent in all three cohorts, and only mildly enriched in two of them.

As can be seen in
Figure~\ref{fig:combined}, the CRISPR cells and the glasso cells occupy the grid almost without meeting.

The glasso values themselves are concentrated near zero. In the CRC cohort, $W$ has a mean of 0.083 and a
standard deviation of 0.182, 57\% of the observed cells are exactly zero and only 5\% exceed 0.5
(Figure~\ref{fig:wdist}), and the other two cohorts behave the same way. A regression on the continuous $W$
therefore faces a heavily zero-inflated target. For this reason we also treat the thresholded
version as a separate binary task.

The CRISPR network is concentrated on a small number of bacteria. Its structure is described here on the full grid
rather than on the mask, since the mask block truncates the most connected genera. The median genus carries two or
three spacer matches, while the most connected genus carries 54 in CRC, 124 in IBD and 156 in GvHD. Also, in every
cohort the ten most connected genera account for about 60\% of all CRISPR edges. On the other side, the viruses are
far more even, with a median of two matches per vOTU and a maximum between 8 and 12. Therefore, the bipartite network
is highly asymmetric: a few bacteria are linked to many phages, but hardly any phage is linked to many
bacteria (Figure~\ref{fig:app-ymatrix}).

Finally, both feature matrices are sparse, and the size asymmetry reported in Section~\ref{data:procs} is
measured there over all profiled entities rather than over the masked pairs
(Figures~\ref{fig:app-featcounts} and~\ref{fig:app-featactivity}).

How these features are selected, transformed and combined into a representation of a pair is described in
Section~\ref{methods}.
